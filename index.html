NEW TOOL DETECTS HEART DISEASE 'IN SECONDS'

Superhuman tool could help change the trajectory of the condition for millions of people
BY GAVIN HAINES
The new tool scans electrocardiograms (ECGs) for information that human cardiologists are unable to see.

Software that can detect heart disease in seconds could help change the trajectory of the condition for millions of people, experts said this week.

The new tool, developed by Imperial College London, scans electrocardiograms (ECGs) for information that human cardiologists are unable to see.

“We looked at the ECGs to see if we could do things that are superhuman,” said Imperial’s Dr Arunashis Sau. “Not things that clinicians can already do, but things that no cardiologist, no matter how expert, can do.”

A trial involving 67,000 patients found that the tool successfully correctly identified up to 81% of patients who had heart failure and 90% of those who had heart valve disease.

WE'RE LIKELY TO BREACH 1.5C. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT IS STILL IN OUR HANDS

Crossing the climate threshold now looks unavoidable, but temperatures are still shaped by the choices we make now

A new UN report says crossing the Paris climate threshold now looks unavoidable, but how far temperatures rise, how long they stay there and whether they can eventually be brought back down are still shaped by the choices we make now

For almost a decade, keeping global warming below 1.5C has been one of the defining goals of global climate action. Now, for the first time, the UN Environment Programme (UNEP) has concluded that the world is likely to breach that threshold within the next few years, while also making clear that crossing it does not mean temperatures have to remain there or that further efforts to limit warming become futile.

Published on Wednesday, UNEP's Limiting Overshoot report describes what it calls an "overshoot, peak and decline" pathway, in which temperatures rise beyond 1.5C before peaking as greenhouse gas emissions fall towards net zero, then begin to decline if humanity succeeds in removing more carbon dioxide from the atmosphere than it releases.

Under the most ambitious scenario examined by UNEP, warming would peak at around 1.8C by the middle of the century. By contrast, policies currently in place would put the world on course for around 2.6C of warming by 2100, according to the report, which underlines why every fraction of a degree avoided continues to reduce the scale of harm.

US-INDIA EXPAND COOPERATION ON UNDERSEA CABLE NETWORKS

India and the United States have expanded cooperation on undersea cable networks under the US-India TRUST (Transforming the Relationship Utilising Strategic Technologies) Initiative. The partnership covers subsea systems that carry more than 95% of global digital traffic and support international communications, financial services, cloud computing, and the global digital economy.

US-India TRUST Initiative

TRUST stands for Transforming the Relationship Utilising Strategic Technologies. It is a bilateral framework between India and the United States for cooperation in strategic technologies, including secure digital infrastructure and resilient supply chains. The undersea cable partnership falls within this framework and involves government and industry stakeholders.

Undersea Cable Networks

Undersea or submarine cables are fibre-optic cables laid on the seabed for long-distance data transmission. These cables connect continents and form the backbone of global internet connectivity, banking transactions, cloud services, and cross-border communications. Their security is linked to physical protection, route diversity, and landing-station resilience.
